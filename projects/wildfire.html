Wildfire Impact & Vegetation Change Detection

ArcGIS Pro • Landsat 8 • NDVI • NBR • Change Detection

Project Overview

This remote-sensing project examined landscape and vegetation changes associated with the 2021 wildfire near Lillooet and Lytton, British Columbia. Landsat 8 imagery from July 29, 2020, and September 2, 2021, was used to compare pre-fire and post-fire conditions.

True-colour and false-colour infrared composites were produced to visually compare changes in vegetation and surface conditions. NDVI was used to evaluate changes in vegetation health, while NBR and Delta NBR were used to identify fire-affected areas and differences in burn severity.

Project Objectives

  • Compare pre-fire and post-fire Landsat imagery.
  • Produce true-colour and false-colour infrared composites.
  • Evaluate vegetation change using NDVI.
  • Identify wildfire impacts and burn severity using NBR.
  • Present the findings in four professional map layouts.

Methods and Tools

Landsat 8 scenes were obtained for WRS-2 Path 47, Row 25. Spectral bands were combined to create true-colour and false-colour infrared composites for both dates.

NDVI rasters were calculated to assess changes in vegetation health. NBR and Delta NBR were used to highlight fire-affected areas and classify differences between pre-fire and post-fire landscapes.

Skills Demonstrated

Landsat image processing, spectral-band combinations, raster calculations, NDVI analysis, NBR analysis, wildfire change detection, burn-impact interpretation, cartographic design, and remote-sensing communication.

Project Outcome

The analysis highlighted substantial vegetation loss and fire-related landscape change around Lytton and nearby valleys. The false-colour and index-based maps made it possible to compare healthy vegetation, stressed areas, exposed ground, and zones affected by the wildfire.
