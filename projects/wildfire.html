Wildfire Impact & Vegetation Change Detection

ArcGIS Pro • Landsat 8 • NDVI • NBR • Change Detection

Project Overview

This remote-sensing project examined landscape and vegetation changes associated with the 2021 wildfire near Lillooet and Lytton, British Columbia. Landsat 8 imagery from July 29, 2020, and September 2, 2021, was used to compare conditions before and after the wildfire.

True-colour and false-colour infrared composites were created to visually compare changes in vegetation, exposed ground, and surface conditions. NDVI was used to assess changes in vegetation health, while NBR and Delta NBR were used to identify fire-affected areas and differences in burn impact.

Project Objectives

  • Compare pre-fire and post-fire Landsat 8 imagery.
  • Produce true-colour and false-colour infrared composites.
  • Assess vegetation change using NDVI.
  • Identify wildfire impacts using NBR and Delta NBR.
  • Present the findings in four professional map layouts.

Methods and Tools

Landsat 8 scenes for WRS-2 Path 47, Row 25 were assembled for the two study dates. Spectral bands were combined in ArcGIS Pro to create true-colour and false-colour infrared composites.

NDVI rasters were calculated to compare vegetation condition before and after the wildfire. NBR and Delta NBR were used to identify areas with reduced vegetation and stronger fire-related change.

Skills Demonstrated

Landsat image processing, spectral-band combinations, raster calculations, NDVI analysis, NBR and Delta NBR analysis, wildfire change detection, cartographic design, map interpretation, and remote-sensing communication.

Project Outcome

The analysis highlighted substantial vegetation loss and fire-related landscape change near Lytton and surrounding areas. The false-colour, NDVI, and NBR products helped distinguish healthy vegetation, stressed vegetation, exposed ground, and locations affected by the wildfire.
