Forest Regeneration Priority Analysis

QGIS • GeoPackage • Spatial Database • Buffering • Difference Analysis

Project Overview

This environmental GIS project identified priority areas for forest regeneration in Southwest Middlesex and Newbury, Ontario. Roads, waterbodies, natural-heritage features, and study-area boundaries were integrated into a spatial database and processed in QGIS.

Buffering, centroid generation, field calculations, difference analysis, and dissolving were used to distinguish high- and medium-priority regeneration areas. The final map presented the resulting priority zones together with existing natural-heritage features.

Project Objectives

  • Integrate roads, waterbodies, natural heritage, and study-area data.
  • Reproject all required datasets into a common coordinate system.
  • Identify potential locations for forest-interior expansion.
  • Distinguish high- and medium-priority regeneration areas.
  • Exclude areas affected by nearby roads.

Methods and Tools

Data was imported into a connected spatial database using QGIS DB Manager. Required layers were reprojected and clipped to the Southwest Middlesex and Newbury study area.

A 100-metre buffer was created around natural-heritage features. Centroids and calculated-radius buffers were used to identify opportunities for increasing forest interior. Difference and dissolve tools were then applied to produce the final high- and medium-priority areas after accounting for a 30-metre road buffer.

Skills Demonstrated

QGIS spatial-database management, DB Manager, reprojection, clipping, buffering, centroid generation, field calculations, difference analysis, dissolving, environmental suitability analysis, and thematic cartography.

Project Outcome

The project produced a final woodland-priority map showing high- and medium-priority forest regeneration areas. The results demonstrated how GIS can support habitat expansion, woodland connectivity, and environmental land-management decisions.
