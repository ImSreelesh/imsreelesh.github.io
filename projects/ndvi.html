NDVI & Land-Cover Classification

ArcGIS Pro • Colour-Infrared Imagery • NDVI • Segmentation • Supervised Classification

Project Overview

This remote-sensing project compared vegetation distribution and land-cover characteristics in the Stoney Creek and Longwoods planning districts of London, Ontario. The two areas were selected because they represent contrasting suburban and rural landscapes.

Colour-infrared imagery was clipped to each study area. NDVI was used to assess vegetation greenness, while image segmentation and supervised classification were used to identify impervious and pervious land-cover classes.

Project Objectives

  • Compare vegetation distribution in two contrasting planning districts.
  • Calculate NDVI to evaluate vegetation greenness and relative biomass.
  • Segment the imagery into groups of spectrally similar pixels.
  • Classify impervious and pervious land-cover surfaces.
  • Compare urbanized and rural land-cover patterns.

Methods and Tools

Colour-infrared imagery was clipped using the Stoney Creek and Longwoods planning-district boundaries. NDVI was calculated using the near-infrared and red bands to represent vegetation condition.

Image segmentation was completed before creating supervised training samples. Buildings, roads, sidewalks, and paved surfaces were classified as impervious, while vegetation, grass, water, and unpaved surfaces were classified as pervious.

Skills Demonstrated

Colour-infrared image interpretation, raster clipping, NDVI calculation, image segmentation, supervised classification, training-sample creation, raster reclassification, raster-to-vector conversion, land-cover statistics, and cartographic design.

Project Outcome

The analysis demonstrated clear differences between the two study areas. Stoney Creek contained more developed and impervious land cover, while Longwoods retained more natural vegetation and pervious surfaces. The results illustrated how remote sensing can support urban planning and environmental assessment.
