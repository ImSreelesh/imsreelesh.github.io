Rail Derailment Emergency Evacuation Planning

ArcGIS Pro • ModelBuilder • Network Analyst • Location-Allocation

Project Overview

This project developed two GIS-based public-safety scenarios for a simulated train derailment in London, Ontario. The first scenario allocated population located within the mandatory evacuation zone to suitable shelters without crossing the railway corridor near the accident site.

The second scenario examined how Zones 1 and 2 could act as barriers to non-emergency travel during the immediate response and clean-up stages. London Transit bus routes were reconstructed to identify routes affected or diverted by the hazard zones.

ArcGIS Pro, ModelBuilder, Network Analyst, and location-allocation tools were used to build the network dataset, combine shelter locations, model population demand, automate shelter allocation, and evaluate transportation disruption.

Project Objectives

  • Identify population demand points within the mandatory evacuation zone.
  • Combine schools and community centres into a shelter feature class.
  • Allocate evacuees to the closest suitable shelters without crossing the railway corridor.
  • Use Zones 1 and 2 as barriers to non-emergency transportation.
  • Compare regular London Transit routes with routes diverted by the hazard zones.
  • Automate the location-allocation workflow using ModelBuilder.

Methods and Tools

Schools and community centres were combined to create potential evacuation shelters. Statistics Canada Dissemination Block centroids and population counts were used as demand points.

A network dataset was generated from the single-line road dataset. Network restrictions were applied so evacuees could not cross the railway corridor near the accident site. Location-allocation analysis was then used to assign population demand to suitable shelters.

Zones 1 and 2 were also applied as barriers to London Transit routes to evaluate transportation disruption and identify diverted routes. ModelBuilder was used to automate the shelter-allocation portion of the workflow.

Skills Demonstrated

Network dataset creation, location-allocation analysis, demand and facility modelling, network barriers, transit-route analysis, ModelBuilder automation, spatial-data integration, emergency planning, and academic poster design.

Project Outcome

The project produced four maps showing evacuation zones and shelters, population-to-shelter allocation, regular London Transit routes, and routes diverted by the hazard barriers. The final workflow demonstrated how GIS can support shelter planning, transportation-impact assessment, and emergency communication following a rail accident.
